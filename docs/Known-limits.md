# Known limits

What NibKeep does not do yet, or does imperfectly, beyond what the [specifications](spec/README.md) and the [technical design](Technical-design.md) already say. Each is a place for later work.

They are grouped by what can be done about them, and each says how it could be mitigated, where a way is known:

1. [Systems, devices and libraries](#1-systems-devices-and-libraries): what an operating system, a device, a library or another service imposes. NibKeep can only work around them.
2. [Trade-offs we accept](#2-trade-offs-we-accept): what follows from devices that trust each other and write apart with no server, or from a choice made on purpose. A fix would cost something worth more; a mitigation makes them rarer or visible.
3. [Our architecture](#3-our-architecture): what NibKeep's own design or code does slowly, holds up or gets wrong, and could do better without giving anything up.
4. [Not built yet](#4-not-built-yet): features with nothing in their way.
5. [Tests](#5-tests): tests that fail now and then, and what no test covers.

A mitigation that would change which changes count, or what the formats hold, says so: the formats are at [version 1](Plan.md#version-1-of-the-formats), so it needs a new version.

The first two groups stay, however much is built: their mitigations make them rarer or visible, not gone. Progress shows in groups 3 to 5, and in the plan's lists of [what comes before each stage of daily use](Plan.md#known-limits-before-daily-use), which give the order of work. A limit that a fix narrows is written anew, in the group where it then belongs.

## Mitigations that several limits share

One piece of work closes each of these sets, so it is built once, rather than a mitigation for each limit:

- **Derived data kept in the state file, and brought up to date from the changes that came,** in place of reading a space whole each time. It closes most of the [work that grows with the library](#work-that-grows-with-the-library): the planner, the counts of `nib status`, a tree's status and its state at a revision, and the walk for a removed member's devices; and what a tree needs of purges ([checking and repair](#checking-and-repair)).
- **An index beside each pack of a backup:** a backup's update that reads every pack whole, and a removal that stands only on a backup's files of their own.
- **A sync that goes on, in the same connection, with what its first round made possible:** a space that reaches another device in two or three syncs, and a member through a group in three or four.
- **Accounts that say more:** sent as what changed since the last, and telling originals from checkouts. Both change what an account holds, so they are one change of its format: every replica that keeps every other's whole account, and a remover that waits for another disk's proof.
- **Checks recorded once for the replica, in a file of their own:** a blob that several spaces share, read once for each, and every check that writes to each space's log file.
- **A burst that commits by itself after a moment of no use:** a burst left open by an idle program, and perhaps the tests of several programs on one replica that fail on Windows.
- **Relays,** in phase 8: the limits under [Relays](#relays), and most of [what a device knows of the others](#what-a-device-knows-of-the-others), which syncing more often narrows.

## 1. Systems, devices and libraries

### Apple's systems

- **A sandboxed Mac app cannot tell that iCloud keeps the Desktop and Documents:** it may not read iCloud Drive's links, so it would make a library there. To do when an app comes to macOS; `nib` in Terminal tells. *Mitigation:* an app has the person choose the library's folder in the system's panel, then asks the system whether that folder is kept in iCloud (`isUbiquitousItem`), which it may answer for a folder the person chose.
- **On macOS there is no login entry yet,** so `nibsync` runs from `nib sync --at-login on` until the person logs out, but reaches the local network only while the Terminal that started it is open: macOS counts it as part of Terminal, lists Terminal as running in the background, and once Terminal quits, lets nothing it sends through, without an error. The keychain also asks for the person's password before `nibsync` may read the key that `nib` put there, as it asks for another program's. *Mitigation:* `nibsync` in a signed app bundle of its own, registered as a login item (`SMAppService`), which macOS counts as a program of its own, with its own access to the local network; and a keychain access group that `nib`, apps and `nibsync` share, signed by one team.
- **macOS and iOS read no drive's name yet** ([where the copies are](Technical-design.md#96-where-the-copies-are)), so there a replica's disk is the computer's, on the drive the system runs from, and a disk of its own elsewhere. *Mitigation:* the volume's UUID, from Disk Arbitration on macOS, and on iOS from the resource values of a folder the person chose, if iOS gives it there.
- **On macOS and iOS, Bonjour chooses the networks it announces on** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#102-finding-replicas)), which may include a VPN's; NibKeep takes in only answers with addresses on home networks, and announces no names. *Mitigation:* register the service on each home network's interface by its index, rather than on every interface.
- **An iPhone's app that leaves the screen during a sync** keeps the sync role until that sync ends, and iOS may end it meanwhile, as it ends a suspended app that holds a lock on a file of a shared container. *Mitigation:* ask iOS for background time while a sync runs, and give up the role and the lock as the app leaves the screen.
- **iOS gives an app 256 open files,** which the tests on an iPhone raise, so they do not show whether an app with many spaces would run out. *Mitigation:* one test at iOS's own limit, and only the files of the spaces in use kept open.

### Android

- **Android gives one developer's apps one name for the phone only when one key signs them,** so two apps signed with different keys count as two disks. *Mitigation:* one signing key for every build, debug builds too.

### Windows

- **Windows names a drive only by its partition table:** a drive without one, or an MBR whose signature is zeros, is named there by its file system's serial, where Linux names it by its WWN or serial. *Mitigation:* ask the drive for its serial (`IOCTL_STORAGE_QUERY_PROPERTY`), as Linux reads it.
- **A backup on Windows behind a network path without a drive's letter** is found only at the folder where it was last found, or with `nib backup update --at`. *Mitigation:* remember each such path where a backup was found, as Windows lists no shares to look in.
- **Windows asks** whether an app may take connections the first time it syncs or adds a device; a holder that the person did not allow still connects to the others. Where the person refuses the rule that `nib` adds, the copy that starts makes Windows ask about `nibsync.exe` itself: allowing it adds a rule for that program, on the networks the person ticks, and cancelling blocks it. `nib` does not tell the person that the network is public, where the firewall's rule does not apply, nor that a program they blocked stays blocked. *Mitigation:* read the network's category (`INetworkListManager`) and the firewall's rules (`INetFwPolicy2`), and say what keeps syncing from working.
- **`nibsync --version` typed in a terminal on Windows prints nothing,** as `nibsync` has no console there, so that no window opens at login. Through a pipe it answers, as programs that ask for its version read it: `nibsync --version | Write-Output`. *Mitigation:* attach to the terminal's console when started from one; the answer then comes after the next prompt, as the shell does not wait for a program without a console.
- **Closing the console window ends `nib` before it clears a copied secret;** Ctrl+C clears. *Mitigation:* Windows waits, for about five seconds, until the routine that handles a closed console returns, but the `ctrlc` crate's routine returns at once: a routine of `nib`'s own could clear before it returns.

### Every system

- **Copying a secret** (`nib secret copy`): the clipboard is cleared if it still holds the same text, so of two copies of one secret, the first one's clear ends the second's time; Linux has no change counter to tell them apart, and `arboard` shows none. *Mitigation:* Windows and macOS have counters (`GetClipboardSequenceNumber`, `changeCount`); on Linux, the program that copied can keep serving the clipboard, which tells it when another program takes it, and clear only while it still holds it.
- **A phone whose ID does not read,** as after an error of the keystore or the keychain, takes its own library for a copy, and an app then offers to make the device from it, which revokes the phone's own device: only the person's answer guards that. *Mitigation:* tell an error reading the ID from an ID that is missing, and on an error offer only to try again.
- **mdns-sd joins the mDNS group on every interface as it starts,** a cable to the provider and a VPN's too, for the moment before it takes the choice of home networks; it sends nothing there, but the network sees the membership. It has no way to start with no interface yet. *Mitigation:* such a way, offered to mdns-sd.
- **An original edited within the second its modification time records, keeping its size,** is reported by the regular check as possibly damaged. *Mitigation:* as Git does, count a file whose recorded time is as late as the check that recorded it as edited, not damaged, when its content differs; and keep nanoseconds where the file system has them.
- **The second read that keeps one bad read from being taken for damage may come from the system's cache** rather than from the disk. *Mitigation:* drop the file from the cache before reading it again (`posix_fadvise` on Linux, `F_NOCACHE` on macOS, an unbuffered read on Windows).
- **Computers make thumbnails only of JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, BMP and TIFF,** so HEIC and HEIF, AVIF, JPEG XL, camera RAW and videos added from a computer have no thumbnail on any device; the decoders that Windows and macOS bring could make them there. Copies are linked only between photos whose thumbnails have a picture hash, so not those that no device made a thumbnail of. *Mitigation:* those decoders, and on Linux libheif and FFmpeg, once their licences and patents are checked.
- **A thumbnailer left to finish alone after a minute keeps its thread,** and a core, until the program ends, as a thread cannot be stopped from outside; `nib` ends after each command, but an app would not. *Mitigation:* decode in a child process, which can be ended, and which also keeps a crafted picture away from the program's memory.
- **A JPEG cut short gets a flat grey thumbnail,** as the decoder reads what there is of it without an error, and that thumbnail is kept and synced as any other. *Mitigation:* make none of a JPEG without its end marker.
- **Two computers that update one backup on a share at once** are kept apart by the lock of the share's file system, which some NFS servers do not keep. *Mitigation:* try the lock as the backup is added, and refuse a share that does not keep it.
- **A clone's folder is checked and made, then Git clones into it:** a link that another program puts in place of one of its parent folders between the two would take the clone through it. **A move checks its place, then renames:** what another program puts at the place between the two is replaced, as no portable call renames without replacing. *Mitigation:* each system's own call that renames without replacing (`renameat2` with `RENAME_NOREPLACE` on Linux, `renamex_np` with `RENAME_EXCL` on macOS, `MoveFileExW` without `MOVEFILE_REPLACE_EXISTING` on Windows), and a clone into a new hidden folder, renamed into place so.

### Data from other services

- **Takeout names its files in ways that are not written down:** an edited copy finds its original's JSON file only by the marks of the languages Google Photos uses most, and otherwise comes with what it says of itself; a sidecar file that Takeout names some other way than those known matches only by its title; and an edited copy is linked only to an original in its own folder, and not when Takeout cut its name. *Mitigation:* more languages' marks as they turn up, and a sidecar matched by the photo's own time and size too.
- **`nib file add --from` records a folder's path in its source from the folder given on,** not from where the phone kept it, which it does not know.

## 2. Trade-offs we accept

Devices write apart, each trusts the others it was paired with, and no server orders what they do. The [technical design](Technical-design.md#65-which-changes-count) weighs each hole in which changes count, and accepts those whose fix would cost more than it saves.

### Trust between devices

- **A replica that was still trusted** can plant statements, dated early, that outlast its revocation ([technical design](Technical-design.md#11-revocation-and-new-keys) and the [identity specification](spec/identity.md) say so), such as replicas of its own that it listed, which stay until they are revoked too; a revoked replica that held an old key can stop it from being given out. Roles, which came in phase 6, are what can limit this. It cannot list a key on another replica's record, nor state an encryption key for another, as only a key's own listing lists it. *Mitigation:* as a replica is revoked, offer to revoke the replicas it listed too, and show for review its changes dated earlier than the changes they build on, which is how statements dated early look.
- **A replica that holds the secrets can fill the few places kept for a statement's record keys** with keys that open to other hashes than the sealing's: they are kept, as another change may seal the statement too ([Log](spec/log.md#5-payloads-and-secret-fields)), and a key that comes after is refused until the next sync. Only a replica with the secrets can wrap such keys.
- **A secret statement's entity is sealed with it,** so a role cannot be judged by it: an editor's secret statement counts on any entity, a viewer's on none, and so does an editor's retraction of secret statements, which replacing one writes: an editor could replace a secret value on another member's record, though NibKeep keeps none there. Accepted: hiding which entity's secret changed is the point.
- **A replica that turned bad before it was revoked** can have given another a space's entry with a first key that is not the space's: that device then takes the space but cannot read it, as the entry it took first wins. A later engine can try another entry when the space does not open, without a new rule. The first change that the space takes is pinned by the entry it took, so only a replica that gives entries can plant one of its own making, under the space's ID: one that opens with its own key, which no such check finds ([technical design](Technical-design.md#138-groups)).
- **A revocation from a device added after the revoked one lasts only until the revoked device revokes that one back,** whenever it does: so that two revocations that cross meet on every device, a device still hears a revoked one that would stay by revoking back those that revoked it ([technical design](Technical-design.md#11-revocation-and-new-keys)), and a thief who holds an unlocked device revoked so can take it back at any time, rather than only before the others hear of the revocation. A revocation from a device added before it is final, and the device it revokes back shows that it was revoked, which the person did not do. Without this, a device that took an infected device's revocation of another before that one's revocation of the infected device refused the other for good, and kept syncing with the infected device. *Mitigation:* `nib device revoke` says so when the device revoked was added before this one, and names the devices added before it, to revoke it from one of them too.
- **A device that holds its own revocation without its payload does not know that it was revoked,** as when the revocation never reached it: a device that turned bad may send it so, and the devices that read the revocation send a revoked device nothing. It builds its revocations on no governing change that it cannot read, so it still stays by revoking back the device that revoked it, if that device was added after it ([technical design](Technical-design.md#11-revocation-and-new-keys)), once the person sees on another device that it was revoked. *Mitigation:* a device that holds a governing change it cannot read, after a sync that should have brought its payload, says that another device may have revoked it.
- **Every governing change is wrapped with the space's first key,** which a revoked or removed device keeps: one that gets later governing changes, as from a device that has not heard of its revocation, reads who the devices and members are by their keys and IDs, their roles, choices and acceptances. Governing changes hold no names, addresses or data. Accepted: so every device reads every governing change that it holds, and none decides otherwise for want of a key, which a device that turned bad could cause by giving a new key to some devices and not to others.
- **What a revoked device's purge destroyed comes back a sync late:** a device that applied the purge before it held the revocation sets the purge aside at the sync that brings the revocation, and gets back what the purge destroyed only at its next sync with a device that kept it, such as the revoker, which never applied it. Until then it shows those values gone. *Mitigation:* a sync that sets a purge aside reconciles the space again before it ends.
- **A removed member's device that holds its removal can still send changes that build on none of it,** such as changes dated before it: they are set aside for review, as nothing records which removed devices hold their removal, and the member's role decides nothing of them. Nor do they get keys or files of what they had that they lacked, such as the content of a file whose change reached them before its blob.
- **A key that a member made while being removed,** if someone who left states another key under its number, is taken by nobody who lacks it, as the two do not agree and neither counts: what was written with it is then unreadable to those who join later. Accepted: only what the member wrote unknowing, which is set aside, needs that key, so the cost is that admins cannot review it. Taking the earliest statement's key instead would let a replica that dates its change early plant a key of its own.
- **In a space with members, a change that a replica made before it held its own listing there** counts for nothing, as its key had no member yet. NibKeep's own replicas write there only once they hold their listing.
- **A space's names, on a device that does not hold it, are those that any device of the person's wrote last** in the personal space: one that turned against its user can show a space there under another name, until a device that holds it writes the space's own names back after its next sync. A device that holds a space shows the space's own names.
- **A space reaches a group's members only from an admin of the group:** an entry that gives a space in a group counts only from an admin there, so only an admin's device that holds the space gives it. A space that only other members of the group hold, as when its admin is an editor in the group and no admin of the group is in it, reaches nobody through the group, though the group agreed to join, until an admin of the group is in the space: its admin invites one, or the group's admin makes them an admin of the group. The [technical design](Technical-design.md#138-groups) says why. So too, **a recovery code reaches a space through a group only once an admin of the group is in it,** as a member's devices do: the group's entry that gives a code the space comes from an admin's device that holds it.
- **Two groups that each hold the other,** which two admins can make at once, give nothing through the cycle, and no device anchors either in the other: a member of one acts in the other only through a third space or group that holds both. An admin takes one of them out to mend it.
- **An AI agent that writes its changes by other means than NibKeep's programs** can take statements away from the library's records, such as a type's field or a policy's number, and delete, restore or merge such records, or a Git repository's, which its own replica refuses to do ([technical design](Technical-design.md#14-ai-agents)): every replica decides from the payload alone whether an AI agent's change counts, and a retraction names only the statements it takes away. Undone by stating them again, and a replica states its own endpoint again at its next sync. So a clone trusts a repository whose record, as it is, a person made, though such an agent took away part of it, such as its pin or its deletion; the addresses are the person's still. Any AI agent may take values away from the person's data, and delete it, which history shows. *Mitigation:* history offers to undo what an agent took away. A rule for every replica would need retractions to name their fields, a change of the formats before version 1, and was not taken.
- **A thief who steals and unlocks a waiting archive node** reads what waits there until its wait ends.
- **Android's decoders read a picked file before the engine does,** so a crafted picture that the person picks reaches them; one that came from another device never does.

### Devices that write apart

- **A value written in plain on a device that did not know yet that its field had become secret for good** is set aside once the two devices sync, as only values written before the field became secret count in plain ([technical design](Technical-design.md#12-secrets)), and no review can accept it. Nothing writes it again, sealed: the person enters it again. *Mitigation:* an app on a device that holds the secrets shows the person the value set aside, and offers to store it sealed.
- **A new secrets key waits for a device that holds the secrets to unlock them:** until one does after a revocation, new secret values are sealed with a key that the revoked device holds, though inside changes sealed with the space's new key, which it lacks. *Mitigation:* after a revocation, ask the person, on a device that holds the secrets, to unlock them once.
- **A code typed back on two devices before they sync** has for its maker the one whose statement comes first: secrets that the other alone gave it do not come back, and `nib recovery list` says, once the two have synced, that it holds none.
- **A code brings back what its source holds,** a backup or a library on the computer that no longer unlocks: a space or group that the source does not hold does not come back, as nothing else holds it for the new device. *Mitigation:* backups kept up to date; later, recovering through another member's device, and relays that hold the recovery file ([technical design](Technical-design.md#111-recovery-codes)).
- **Copies of one content that two replicas sealed apart,** before either learned of the other's keys, are settled within one space alone, on the one with the smallest sealed hash ([technical design](Technical-design.md#79-blobs)). Across spaces both stay: a copy that another space of the replica keeps a key for is kept, as that space may lack the smaller one's key.
- **Of two thumbnails of one content,** the one not shown stays while the device that made it never syncs again, as only its maker purges it.
- **Two devices that settle two files at one path apart,** each deleting the copy of the other's file, delete both from the tree, as each asked; `nib restore` brings either back.
- **A copy beside renamed and edited is a new file,** and the other file is deleted, as only a copy renamed as it was written is taken for that file moved: the content stays, and the old file keeps its history. So is a copy edited and committed, when a crash cut the commit short before it recorded the edit, then renamed before the next commit.

### What a device knows of the others

A device knows of the others what its last syncs told it. Syncing more often, as syncing from login does, and relays later, narrow these.

- **An account counts what a replica held at its last sync,** and a mark shows elsewhere only after two syncs ([where the copies are](Technical-design.md#96-where-the-copies-are)).
- **A device that a revoked one added stays in the spaces shared with others** until each of the person's other remaining replicas, another app's on the same computer too, has synced since the revocation, as one of them may have paired it ([technical design](Technical-design.md#11-revocation-and-new-keys)): one that never syncs again, as a pairing cut short or an app never opened, keeps it there. *Mitigation:* revoke what is no longer used.
- **A device counts the backups whose records it holds:** one that has not yet heard of a backup made on another computer may revoke a code that a newer one replaces, though that backup carries the old code alone.
- **A code that a newer one replaces is revoked only by a device that learns that every backup carries the newer one:** after a sync or an update of a backup. One whose devices all stay off keeps it standing, and its paper working, meanwhile.
- **A device listed by a code introduces itself only in the syncs it starts,** as the others do not know its endpoint yet: a device of the person's that it kept, or another member's, takes it once it connects, or from a backup, and not before.
- **`nib purge` does not name the backups that hold purged content** until they are updated next ([backups](Technical-design.md#910-backups)).
- **Copies that wait** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#911-copies-that-wait)): a purge names other members' copies that wait as their latest accounts say, which may be weeks old, and a backup that has never been updated after its wait was set with the wait marked; a purge of what a move left behind waits on the old space's archive nodes as any purge; and stopping a purge gives back only what the waiting copy kept of it, so a purge of a change whose payload that device never had gives nothing back for it.

### Clocks

- **A device decides what a claim to the admin role gives its user now by the latest wall clock time it was given,** so one whose clock runs behind lets its user act as an admin later; the log, which decides by the claimant's timestamps, is not affected ([technical design](Technical-design.md#139-claims-to-the-admin-role)).

### Choices of the design

- **Every command spends about 0.12 s unlocking** with a passphrase (Argon2, by design). With the key in the keystore, a command takes about 25 ms.
- **`nib mcp serve` on an AI agent's own replica holds it open** while it runs, so `nib` cannot use that copy meanwhile; it is the AI agent's alone. On the user's replica, it opens the library for each tool call.
- **The guard for builds under development covers only what cargo starts in the workspace** ([the plan](Plan.md#guards-for-builds-under-development)): a `nib` run straight from `target/debug`, or by `cargo run` from a folder outside the workspace, still looks above the folder it runs in, and takes `NIBKEEP_LIBRARY` from the shell. The agents' rule to use scratch libraries covers those.
- **`nib init --join` does not hold the sync role while it joins,** as no other program has the new replica open yet.
- **A new device tries each device that announces its code's first two digits, lowest ID first** ([sync](spec/sync.md#2-pairing)). Of two pairings at once whose codes share them, 1 in 90, each try of the person whose device sorts later also spends one of the other's three tries, which shows there as a wrong one: two mistyped codes and a right one can spend the other's code. A try that reaches the other device while it runs its first sync waits until that sync ends. *Mitigation:* the other device answers that it is busy while it syncs.
- **In a space other than the personal one, a code's record has no type and no name,** only the statement that names the code, as a viewer may write nothing else there; `nib recovery list` names a code from the personal space.
- **Which devices hold a space is read from their choices** in the personal space, so a device that asked for a space and was not given it yet, or that dropped it and asked again, shows among those that hold it: in `nib space list`, and where a revocation, a recovery or a backup's update names the devices that hold a space it cannot reach. A device that holds the space shows the request, and approving it makes the list true.
- **A revocation names only the spaces that the revoked device chose to hold:** a group's space that it never took, where it is one of the space's replicas through the group ([technical design](Technical-design.md#1310-devices-that-choose-their-spaces)), goes unnamed, and waits for a device of the person's that holds it.
- **A backup's update names the devices that hold each space it did not write, not whether they update that backup:** a drive that only the phone sees keeps what it had of a space that only the desktop holds. *Mitigation:* the devices that update a backup read from its accounts in the personal space, which a backup of the personal space has.
- **A dropped space's secrets keys stay in the secrets compartment** while the secrets are locked as its files are deleted, sealed and opening nothing on the device, until a sync there while they are unlocked deletes them, as the compartment is written only then. `nib sync` and `nibsync` never unlock the secrets; an app that syncs while they are unlocked does.
- **A drop of a space with no files counts its changes against the Critical policy that a space without policies falls back on,** three disks, one away from home, as `nib policy check` does: such a space installs its policies only as its first file is added, or as `nib policy` changes one, after which they can be lowered.
- **A secret file goes from a device that drops its space once one other disk proves its copy,** as a thumbnail and a picture in a field do, since secret files have no storage policy: every replica keeps them, and the drop waits until another device that remains holds the space's secrets.
- **A device paired, or given a space, while another device makes or joins a space** may miss it: consent is given on the device where the person gives it, which gives the new space only to the devices it knows of, and nothing records the consent for the others to act on. The device asks for the space, which the person approves on a device that holds it; `nib space list` shows each space of the person's that the device does not hold.
- **Carried history** shows each value as written at the move in the new space, and replaced then if it was past; the mover's note gives when the old space recorded it, which `nib history` shows and orders by.
- **A move with the old space's secrets locked takes no sealed value of a field that the entity's types do not list,** as which entity a sealed value is of is unknown then, and the move refuses only an entity that may hold secret values by its types or its plain values. Such a value stays with the old entity, and no purge of what stays behind takes it. *Mitigation:* an app asks for the secrets before a move in a space that holds sealed values.
- **Secret values that a move took stay in the old space until a purge sees them:** a purge of what a move left behind takes them only on the mover's device with the secrets of both spaces unlocked, as only then does it see which values moved. `nibsync` and `nib sync` never unlock the secrets, so the rest of what stays is purged after a sync, and the secret values once the mover runs `nib purge --behind`, which unlocks them, or an app syncs while they are unlocked.
- **A file whose policy keeps it in place leaves every store once its original is proven,** after which no other device can fetch it, as only sealed copies travel ([the planner](Technical-design.md#99-the-planner)). So no device fetches a file whose only copies are originals kept in place on other devices, or on a backup.
- **Another member's devices count a backup's copies without its name.**
- **Where groups hold each other, what ended a member's paths through groups takes in the changes on the cycle too:** finding only the records on paths that pass each group once is too costly for some shapes, so a replica takes every record that a walk to the member's group passes. Only two admins acting at once make such a cycle. The secrets may then take a new key that was not needed, and a key that never had a path, as through a group that has not agreed yet, may show as removed until it has one.
- **Empty folders are not kept,** as in Git.
- **Git repositories are read by `nib`**, which runs the person's Git: a program that commits through the engine alone, and not through `nib`, leaves them as they are.
- **The log, diffs and blame leave Git repositories out,** and a picture of the past, written at a revision or as of a moment, clones none.
- **A repository moved or deleted elsewhere stays where it is here,** with the work it may hold: an update lists one moved, for the person to move, and leaves one deleted out of commits until the person deletes or renames it.
- **A tree inside the working tree of another repository,** as one in a home folder that Git keeps, writes there as anywhere: only a repository in the tree's folder or below it is Git's alone.
- **With the secrets locked, a secret file shows no change made elsewhere:** its copy stands for the version it was written or committed at, so `nib status` names no new version from another device, and an update writes it only once the secrets are unlocked ([technical design](Technical-design.md#151-secrets-in-trees)). A diff between revisions, a picture of the past, and a revert leave secret files out while the secrets are locked.
- **An ordinary file made secret is purged only at a terminal:** `nib commit --secret` asks before it purges the old versions, and with `--json` it lists the files and purges nothing.
- **While the secrets are locked, an app shows every secret field a record's types have,** empty ones too: sealed values do not say which record they are of. Unlocked, the empty ones are left out.

## 3. Our architecture

What NibKeep's own design or code does slowly, holds up or gets wrong. Most need no new rule of the log.

### Work that grows with the library

Each of these reads more as the library grows. Most can keep what they compute in the state file, as derived data that is built again whenever it is lost.

- **Governing changes are decided again in full:** each one that becomes readable decides again from all of them, and a revocation reads every change's header to find the revoked key's changes; opening a library builds the roster for the writing key. Fine with the handful of governing changes a library has; an index by author would help if that grows.
- **Catching up reads the log again whole** (its waiting changes and wrapped keys) whenever another program wrote to a space, rather than only what came after its own mark, as the technical design's section on several programs on one replica has it; quick with the state file, but it grows with the waiting changes and keys. *Mitigation:* read from the mark, as the design has it.
- **Devices keep syncing with a removed member's devices,** which get only what ended them, so each sync with them costs a walk of the space's history from the removal down. *Mitigation:* keep the walk's result until the space's governing changes change.
- **The planner** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#99-the-planner)): each sync, and each pass of removals, works out what the replica keeps by reading every file of every space, and choosing each file's policy, once, and again after changes came, which a library of many files will feel. *Mitigation:* what each file needs, kept by space and brought up to date from the changes that came.
- **Accounts** ([where the copies are](Technical-design.md#96-where-the-copies-are)): every replica keeps every other replica's whole account, about 41 bytes for each blob it holds, and brings its own up to date by reading all it holds, at most once a minute while the space's state stays the same. A page saved makes the other programs that have the space open read its log file again, as any write to it does. The check of the space's changes reads the newest 10,000 changes, and says "or more" beyond. *Mitigation:* accounts sent and kept as what changed since the last.
- **A backup's update reads every pack whole,** about a megabyte for each thousand changes and 2 GB for 50,000 thumbnails, which the holder of the sync role repeats for a drive that stays plugged in an hour after new changes came, or daily ([backups](Technical-design.md#910-backups)). *Mitigation:* an index beside each pack, as Git keeps for its packs, read in place of the pack, which is read whole only as it is checked.
- **Checking the library reads a blob that several spaces share once for each of them,** and each records its own check. The check records of originals and checkouts are kept by space, so a file moved to another space counts as unchecked there until a round reads it. *Mitigation:* checks recorded by the blob, once for the replica.
- **`nib status` reads every file of each space** to count pictures and their thumbnails. *Mitigation:* counts kept in the state file.
- **A search that finds more than a page by words** counts the rest by words alone, as a minimum, and names only the page ([technical design](Technical-design.md#76-search)). Counting still reads the types of every entity found, to leave out origins, checkouts and what the caller filters out: on the desktop, the first 50 of 1,667 take about 10 ms, of which the index takes 2.5. A count from the index alone would cost less, but would no longer be a minimum.
- **A tree's status reads the tree's state whole,** every file's path and version, to tell what changed elsewhere: 0.4 s for 10,000 files on the desktop, beside the copy's own sizes and times. `nib log` reads every statement of every file: 0.4 s for 10,000 files committed twice. *Mitigation:* what changed kept by path, from the changes that came.
- **A large tree outgrows SQLite's cache of the state file,** 32 MiB: 10,000 files committed twice take 41 MB. A program that keeps the library open, as an app does, reads it from the disk again after a large commit, and a status or a log then takes two to three times as long as in a program that opens the library anew, as `nib` does. *Mitigation:* a larger cache that a program that keeps the library open chooses (the [open question](Technical-design.md#20-open-questions) of memory for caches).
- **A tree at a revision is replayed** from the payloads of every change the revision builds on, in the whole space, as no state of the past is kept: quick for a library of a few thousand changes, slower for one with many more. **Leaving out a commit not yet whole** replays the whole space too, as a state without some changes is kept nowhere. **Merging competing versions reads their history:** the payload of each version before them, back to the one they all superseded. *Mitigation:* a tree's state kept for each revision, as derived data.

### Writing and locks

- **Starting a tree of many files is slow:** each file's content is sealed into the store and saved on its own, with its own syncs, about 3 ms a file on the desktop, so 10,000 files take some 45 seconds, and a commit that edits them all some 90, recording each file as a change of its own, in batches of 1,000 ([technical design](Technical-design.md#62-one-entity-per-change)). Sealing many blobs and saving their keys at once would cut it, in the store.
- **A move of many files is one batch,** saved all or none, as the copies are moved already, which holds up the space's other writers until it is saved: a change for each file. *Mitigation:* batches recorded as a commit's parts are, so a crash leaves the rest to take in.
- **A sync beside another program saves item by item:** while another program has a space open, what a sync receives of that space is saved one item at a time, each synchronizing the log file, as batches back to back would keep the other program from writing. On a computer, that is whenever an app is open beside the holder of the sync role. Short pauses between batches would let the other program in, at some cost to both.
- **A burst left open by an idle program:** the changes of a burst commit one by one once another program has the space open, but a program that made a burst while alone, and then keeps the space open without using it, holds the state file's write lock until it uses or closes the space. Another program's change, or its opening of the library, or of a space that the idle program made, waits five seconds meanwhile, and fails. `nib` closes the library after each operation; the holder of the sync role keeps one open, but only reads with it; and `nib mcp` keeps an AI agent's replica, which no other program shares. *Mitigation:* a burst that commits by itself after a moment of no use.
- **Protecting anew holds the key file's lock** while it derives a key from the new passphrase or makes a keystore entry (`Library::protect`, `protect_secrets`): about a second, during which other writers of the key file wait. `set_up_secrets` seals before it locks; protecting anew could too, with a protection prepared apart in `nibkeep-keys`. Worth it if a keystore ever prompts there.
- **Setting up, protecting anew and asking for the secrets with the fingerprint** prompt while an app holds the library open, so its other calls and `nib` wait for the person. Unlocking does not: it releases the key first (`Library::secrets_key`). *Mitigation:* the same for the others.
- **A program that keeps the library open seals with its old secrets key** until its secrets settle again, after another program of the same replica made a new one. *Mitigation:* take the new key as the program catches up with the other's writes.
- **`nib file add` makes a round's thumbnails once it has sealed the round's files,** reading each picture again, rather than while it seals the next ones, so adding photos takes about 1.7 times as long as without thumbnails; making them meanwhile would bring it near the longer of the two.
- **An import from Takeout reads each tgz part twice,** decompressing it each time. *Mitigation:* one pass that keeps the sidecars it meets until their photos come, or the other way round.

### Checking and repair

- **Regular checks** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#97-checking-and-repair)) run only in the program that holds the sync role: a library that only `nib` opens, with neither `nib sync --serve` nor syncing from login, is checked only by `nib verify`, whose reading `nib status` does not count, so it says "Checks: none yet" right after, and its copies stop counting 8 weeks after their last check. *Mitigation:* `nib verify` records what it read as checks.
- **Log files and state files are not checked regularly, and a damaged log file is not repaired from other replicas.** *Mitigation:* both in the regular checks; a damaged log cut where its damage starts, and the changes it lost fetched again, by their hashes, from another replica; a damaged state file built again, as it holds only what the log does.
- **A read that fails is tried again only in the next round, and not reported.** *Mitigation:* report it, and try again within the round.
- **A damaged copy moved aside takes its space on the disk** until an intact copy replaces it or its content is purged, and nothing reads it meanwhile, even where most of it is intact. A copy moved aside after a bad read goes back as the next round begins.
- **Each check that records something writes to the log file of each space it read,** so other programs that have the space open read it again, at most every few minutes. *Mitigation:* checks recorded in a file of their own.
- **A damaged thumbnail that the regular check finds is reported without the file it is of.** *Mitigation:* find the file by the thumbnail's content hash.
- **Purged content is known by the copies that held it:** a purge names what it destroys by sealed hashes, so a replica learns which content was purged from its own copies and its own purges. A copy of purged content that the replica never held as a tree's file, such as one made before a purge received from another device, or any copy after the index was lost, is not recognised, and a commit would record it again. **A root whose values another replica restated in a purge** counts only through the index; once the index is lost too, the tree has no folder on this device until it is named again. *Mitigation:* what a tree needs of purges kept in the log's derived state, which comes back with it.
- **Another device's update deletes the old copy before it writes the new file,** when a copy beside was renamed and edited, so a crash between the two leaves that copy with neither until its next update. *Mitigation:* write the new file first.

### Hardening against an infected device

- **Labels come from the user's own replicas,** as all definitions do: one that turns against its user could swap the labels of two fields. `nib` shows the labels that the types give. *Mitigation:* `nib` shows the vocabulary's labels from the program too.
- **A thumbnail that another device made is a JPEG,** which an app may show through decoders written in C, so a device that turned against its user could send a crafted one; the engine could decode and encode every received thumbnail anew in Rust before an app shows it.
- **A device that turned against its user can keep another from removing copies** by asking it to prove them again and again, which holds them there; it cannot make it remove any. *Mitigation:* a limit on how often one device may ask.
- **A backup that an infected device updates may leave out what it should hold,** as that device writes it: its recovery file, so that no code opens it, or changes, so that a recovery brings back less. It cannot make the file name a person or a personal space of its own making, as each code signed, as it was made, what its entry names ([technical design](Technical-design.md#111-recovery-codes)). Two backups guard against it.

### Rules and behaviour

- **Unlocking does not take the keys given:** `Library::unlock_secrets` only opens the compartment; `settle_secrets`, which needs time and randomness, takes them, and `nib` calls it next. *Mitigation:* one call that takes the clock and randomness.
- **A recovered device lists itself in a space with members only on the record that the personal space's entry names,** where the code stands: a code named on the person's record in a group alone, as where the person was made a direct member after the code was carried there, lists it there through the group only. A space that the person has left is taken in as the backup holds it, and lists the new device nowhere.
- **A space reaches another device in two or three syncs:** the first brings its first key through the personal space, the device then asks to be listed, and a device listed there lists it; until then `nib space list` shows it waiting. **A space reaches a member through a group in three or four syncs:** the group's anchor, then an entry in the group, then the space itself, each with another device that holds it; and a few more for each group inside a group on the way, as the member gets each outer group first. *Mitigation:* a sync that goes on, in the same connection, with what its first round made possible.
- **What a role does not allow is saved, then set aside:** the engine refuses beforehand only inviting and giving roles without being an admin; a viewer's change to another's entity is committed, and counts on no device, theirs included. *Mitigation:* the engine refuses beforehand whatever the role does not allow.
- **A move takes no edit that reaches the old space after the move,** which stays there. *Mitigation:* a replica that holds both spaces carries such an edit over to the new entity.
- **What a move leaves behind that holds no content is purged once a sync of the new space with another device ends,** which the mover's replica takes to mean that the device holds the new entity: one that refused its payload, as its disk was nearly full, may not yet. What holds content waits for that content's copies in the new space to be proven ([technical design](Technical-design.md#99-the-planner)). An admin's request is shown where each field the move carried holds one of the old entity's values on the new one, which is looser than the same values, as a field whose last writer wins carries only the value it shows. After the purge, the old entity has no name left, so `nib purge --behind` finds it only by its ID. *Mitigation:* purge once the device says it took the payload.
- **The planner's proofs and removals** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#99-the-planner)): only an origin's original proves, not a checkout's copy, and an account cannot tell the two apart, so a remover that asks a device holding only a checkout waits for another disk's proof. A move takes along the origins and checkouts of the mover's own devices that hold the new space, and purges the others, as those of other members' devices, which it cannot tell apart, and of the mover's devices that do not hold it, as far as their choices have reached the mover's device: such a member who holds the new space too, or such a device that takes it later, or whose choice to hold it came after the move, adds the file again there to have its original counted. A copy that another space of the replica keeps a key for is never removed. Holds and proofs go by clocks: a prover whose wall clock jumps ahead by 10 minutes while a remover counts on it lets its hold go early, and could remove its own copy at once if it removes that file too. *Mitigation:* accounts that tell originals from checkouts; holds measured by a clock that does not jump.
- **A removal stands only on what a backup plugged in holds in files of their own,** larger than 1 MiB, not on the small blobs in its packs ([backups](Technical-design.md#910-backups)). *Mitigation:* the packs' indexes, above, as proof.
- **What waits is destroyed only by the holder of the sync role** ([copies that wait](Technical-design.md#911-copies-that-wait)), so on a device where nothing holds it, as with neither `nib sync --serve` nor syncing from login, it waits until `nib` is next used there after the wait, or longer. `nib purges` lists what waits on a backup as its record says after its last update, and stopping or ending a wait there updates the backup whole. *Mitigation:* any program that opens the library destroys what is due.
- **Copies of a photo are found by a few marks** ([importers](Technical-design.md#912-importers-and-where-files-came-from)): a copy whose time someone wrote by hand, with seconds left out or an offset written another way, or one whose time zone differs from its original's other than to UTC, is not found; a copy cropped, even a little, is not linked; photos of a burst taken within one second, found in different folders, may be linked as copies, which a person removes; a copy's link is to the file it looks most like at the time, never moved to a larger original found later; and a photo whose width and height its metadata lost counts as smaller than one that says them, whatever its pixels. *Mitigation:* links looked at again as larger originals come, and sizes read from the pictures themselves.
- **A move refuses, as it does for a file's content, while a thumbnail of the content it moves has not reached this device;** a sync brings it.
- **Engines of different versions** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#710-separate-apps-on-one-library)): two copies of a program that name themselves alike, such as two builds of `nib`, share one entry in a replica's engines file, which says what the one that opened it last reads. A program installed but never opened is not listed, and an app removed stays listed. *Mitigation:* an entry for each program's path.
- **Taking in a copy** copies its logs into the system's temporary folder while it reads them, which on Arch Linux is in memory. *Mitigation:* a folder inside the library's.
- **A `.nib` owned by another account**, in a folder above, takes over the commands below it (Git's CVE-2022-24765). *Mitigation:* as Git does, refuse a library that another account owns unless the person allows it.
- **A folder in one library's folder, copied there with a `.nib` file that names another library on this computer,** becomes a tree of that other library with `nib init`, as init follows the file of the folder it makes a tree, for a copy of a tree's folder; the commands there then work in that library. *Mitigation:* a library whose folder holds the folder winning over the folder's own file.
- **A login entry that another computer's dotfolders brought** shows in the startup settings beside this computer's, under the same name. *Mitigation:* `nibsync` started from an entry whose replica is not on this computer removes the entry and ends.
- **`nib mcp serve`, on an AI agent's replica, connects as before,** without the sync role.
- **`nib list TYPE --where/--sort FIELD`** also finds fields of the other types that entities of TYPE have (`types::field_of_listed`).
- **Blame and the log order a file's versions by their times,** so of competing versions, made apart on two devices, blame follows the later one alone. *Mitigation:* order them by what each builds on.
- **Executable bits set apart on two devices,** as when one takes the bit away while another takes it away and sets it again, are not listed as competing: the file counts as executable while any of its current bits says so, until a commit sets its bit again. Competing versions are told apart by their content alone, the bit aside. *Mitigation:* the bit among what tells versions apart, so that an update merges it as it merges content.
- **A copy beside another is named after the device** whose replica made the other version, as the space names it, or `elsewhere`; two from devices with one name go to one name, and the second is skipped until the first is gone. *Mitigation:* a short mark of the replica in the name when two devices share one.
- **Two devices that add one path with the same content** still get a copy beside, which the person deletes, as two files at one path are not merged into one. *Mitigation:* an update that finds the same content and bit keeps the file with the smaller ID and deletes the other: an ordinary change, with no new rule.
- **Two devices that change one repository's record apart,** as with two branches checked out at once, record values that compete: a clone takes the one in the statement with the smallest ID, until a device records another. *Mitigation:* `nib status` shows the values that compete, for the person to choose.
- **Competing versions of a secret file merge from nothing,** as their common version is found through ordinary payloads alone, which name no secret statement: the merge marks every line that differs. *Mitigation:* with the secrets unlocked, find the common version through the sealed statements too.

## 4. Not built yet

### Relays

Relays, in phase 8, let devices that never meet on one network sync. Until then:

- **Two users sync only on one network,** as their devices meet in one home, as each user's own devices do, until replicas connect over the Internet. An invitation's code, too, works only there.
- **A move's leftovers that hold content wait for copies in the new space** as many as its policy's count, but without asking for archive nodes' until the new space has one, or for one away from home until relays.
- **A copy whose policy needs one away from home is never removed** until relays come, so a device keeps such files above its size.

### Recovery codes

Of [recovery codes](Technical-design.md#111-recovery-codes), `nib recovery` makes, confirms, lists, checks and revokes a code, which the personal space and the user's other spaces name and give their keys, and `nib recovery restore` brings a library back with it. What falls short is spread over this page by what can be done about it; what is not built yet:

- **A library recovered in place does not take over its old replica's trees' folders, nor its checkouts,** as those count only on the replica that recorded them, which is revoked: a tree is written anew, as on a new computer. *Mitigation:* offer to record the old folders again, by their paths.
- **The secrets of a library that no longer unlocks stay in it** where the code was made without them, although the library's secrets passphrase may still open them: a recovery does not ask for it, as `nib recovery from-copy` does for a copy's secrets.
- **The folder that a recovery in place sets aside is named once,** as the recovery ends: nothing reminds the person later to delete it, nor the keystore entries of its keys. *Mitigation:* `nib status` names it until it is gone.
- **A recovery cut short starts over** when run again, taking in the whole backup again.
- **A code is given the secrets only when asked,** with `nib recovery new --secrets` or `confirm --secrets`; nothing says yet, as a code is made without them, that the device holds secrets that the code will not bring back, beyond the line that says it does not open them.
- **`nib recovery new` names no backup before the code is made,** nor when each was last updated, as the design has it: once the code stands, it names the backups still to update before they carry it.
- **A library comes back on computers alone:** a phone reads no backup yet, and its library is its own, which joins from another device instead.
- **Nothing says where a later sync revoked a device that a lost one had added,** which only a shared space or group listed and none of the person's remaining devices heard of: `nib device revoke` and `nib recovery restore` name the spaces where it happened at once.
- **A space made on a device that is lost before the user's other devices were listed in it** is lost with that device: the others hold its first key, but nobody can list them or revoke the lost one there ([technical design](Technical-design.md#13-sharing)). Where the space names a recovery code of the user's, a later engine can list another device there by the code, under the rule that exists.

### Spaces

- **Joining a space needs a library of one's own in `nib`:** someone new runs `nib init` before `nib space join`.
- **An invitation whose first sync fails** leaves its new member in the space, with the role given and a device that holds nothing of it, until the same device is invited again, which makes it that member again, or an admin removes them.
- **A space that one left stays on each of one's devices,** read only, marked as left in `nib space list`, until `nib space drop` deletes it, one device at a time; its entry stays in the personal space. A device of the user paired after the leaving does not get it, nor does `nib space list` show it on a device that does not hold it. Its files are not checked out, as a checkout is recorded in the space; `nib export` writes them out. Accepted: a device that dropped such a space by itself would let one device that turned against its person, leaving for them, delete the space from all of their devices, and from every device at all where they were its last member.
- **`nib list --json` does not name the spaces it did not look in,** as it prints a list of entities: its text names them, and so does `nib find`, as text and in JSON, as the MCP server's `find` does for an AI agent. *Mitigation:* an object, as `nib find --json` prints, once the forms of the output are reviewed before version 1.
- **A device that cannot open a key sealed to it** says nothing of it yet, so a member who seals another a key that does not open keeps them from what was written with it until the key is given anew, which nothing does yet. A later version can let the device say so, as governing statements that a version does not know are kept.
- **An AI agent's replica always holds the personal space,** besides the spaces chosen for it, though the [technical design](Technical-design.md#13-sharing) lets the person leave the personal space out; that comes in a later step, as every replica holds the personal space now. A space whose admins stop allowing AI agents ends those listed there, with a new key, and allowing them again brings those back, with every key made meanwhile.
- **Pairing gives the personal space's secrets only, and `nib secret give` those of the spaces where the device was listed as it asked;** a holder gives another space's by itself, once the device is listed there and both have unlocked their secrets since.
- **Another member's device asks for a shared space's secrets only once its own are set up,** and only one that holds them gives them, as its person chooses, with `nib`; nothing yet offers them as the member joins.
- **A moved entity's incoming links** stay with the entities that hold them, in the old space: `nib show` shows them on the new entity. A tree does not move yet, nor a folder or a list that holds what is in one.
- **A replica that does not hold the new space of a move,** as an AI agent's, shows the old entity as it was, marked as moved; `nib show` does not show the mark yet.
- **`nib space show` lists only the space's direct members as its members,** and each group's members under the group, with the groups inside it. History names a member through a group as the first group by ID that lists their key names them, of those whose logs the device holds.
- **Only a direct member claims the admin role:** a member through a group alone is made a direct member first ([technical design](Technical-design.md#139-claims-to-the-admin-role)). `nib space show` shows claims; `nib space reject` rejects them.
- **`nib` finds an entity in any space,** but a type, a storage policy or a rule only in the space that `--space` names, or else in the personal space, and `nib export` exports that one space; a new space has the standard vocabulary, not the types the person defined in another.

### Secrets

- **Forgetting a lost secrets' key states nothing** (`Library::forget_lost_secrets`): the other devices still count the phone as holding the secrets until it asks anew, and one that asks it meanwhile waits, and can ask another. A later version can state it, as governing statements that a version does not know are kept ([technical design](Technical-design.md#65-which-changes-count)).
- **Two devices that set up the secrets at the same time,** or two admins a shared space's, before either hears of the other, split them: each device takes the keys of the setup of the one it asked, and cannot read what the other setup's keys sealed. Nothing settles it yet. A later engine can, without a new rule: the person gives the secrets on one device, and the other then passes its own keys back, linked with the first's.

### Files, copies and backups

- **Backups** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#910-backups)): taking a space or a policy off a backup stops writing them, and what it holds of them stays until the folder is made anew. On Android, an app updates a backup only when asked, while it is open, and Android ending the app midway leaves the backup as a pulled drive does; it takes folders only on the drives that Android's own storage lists, not on a network share; the picker synchronizes no folder, so a drive pulled out without unmounting right after an update may lose the files that the update moved into place last, which the next update writes again; and a backup that a phone updated last names its drive by the file system's serial, where a computer may name it by its partition table, so a replica on the same drive counts as another disk until a computer updates the backup. iPhones reach no backup yet. *Mitigation:* a foreground service that holds an update while the app is in the background; naming a backup's drive by its file system's serial on computers too.
- **Only `nib` stops a purge or ends a wait,** on the device itself, and no app yet.
- **Importers** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#912-importers-and-where-files-came-from)): Takeout's favorites and the names of the people Google recognized are left out, though its JSON files hold both; photos in Takeout's trash or archive come in as any other; a Live Photo's picture and video come in as two files.
- **Thumbnails** ([technical design](Technical-design.md#98-thumbnails)): on Android, an app makes them of the photos and videos it adds, with the phone's decoders, for the formats that the phone reads; on an iPhone, nothing makes them yet. `nib file thumbnails` makes thumbnails of files' current versions only, and of nothing that a tree holds.

### Trees

- **A revision has no name,** so lists show it by its type; `nib log` and `nib show` show its message.
- **Other versions written beside a copy pile up** under new names, `NAME.conflict-DEVICE-2` and on, as an update never writes over one, and so do the edits that restores keep, `NAME.orig-2` and on: the person deletes those they no longer need. *Mitigation:* `nib status` lists them, and offers to delete them.
- **A folder whose `.git` is a file,** as a worktree's or a submodule's, is left out and recorded as nothing, as what it holds is elsewhere.
- **A Git repository with other remotes where a tree records one,** as another project cloned at that path, is taken as the tree's when the tree is written into its folder: nothing is cloned or said, and the next commit records its remotes as the repository's. Something there that holds no repository is listed as not cloned. *Mitigation:* `nib clone` asking Git for the remotes of each repository there, and listing one that shares none with the record.
- **A library's folder that moves** leaves the `.nib` files of its trees outside it naming the old folder ([technical design](Technical-design.md#15-trees)). A command in such a tree's folder says so, and `nib init FOLDER`, run in the library's new folder, writes the file again, one tree at a time. *Mitigation:* the library, opened in its new folder, writing the files of its trees on this device.

### Types and vocabulary

- **The standard vocabulary in existing spaces** is not brought up to date; that needs migrations, whose IDs and note are settled, but which nothing runs yet ([technical design](Technical-design.md#57-migrations)).
- **Qualifiers that types define** are not possible yet.
- **A record's fields are ordered by label,** after the vocabulary's: types keep no order of their fields, so a card imported from another program loses its template's order.

### AI agents

- **An AI client on the user's replica works in the personal space alone:** `nib mcp enable` takes no `--in SPACE` yet ([technical design](Technical-design.md#14-ai-agents)). *Mitigation:* pair an AI agent's replica with `--in` for a shared space.
- **`nib mcp enable` tells Claude Code and Codex alone,** through their own commands: Claude Desktop's chats, Cursor, Gemini CLI and the rest take the line that `--print` gives, pasted by the person.
- **An AI client's session that runs already sees a library's server only once it starts anew,** as clients load their servers when a session starts.
- **An AI agent's replica holds the names of every space of the person's,** as the personal space keeps them for the person's devices ([identity](spec/identity.md#8-spaces-of-ones-own)), which every AI agent's replica holds: a program of the AI agent's that reads its replica's files reads the names of the spaces not chosen for it. NibKeep's own tools name to an AI agent only the spaces its replica holds. *Mitigation:* an AI agent's replica without the personal space, which a later step allows (above).
- **An AI client is named as it names itself,** so two clients that give one name share a key, and history tells them apart no further.

### The command line

- **Without a terminal, a passphrase that `nib` has to ask for** fails with the system's error, such as `Device not configured (os error 6)` on macOS, instead of pointing to `--passphrase-file`.
- **Errors with `--json` are still text**, on standard error.
- **A new library's person is named after the account where the system holds no full name,** as on many Linux desktops, whose accounts leave it empty, and on Windows outside a domain, where the system gives programs no full name. *Mitigation:* the person renames it, in `nib edit`; `nib init` could ask, on a terminal.

## 5. Tests

### Tests that fail now and then

- **The library's power-cut test died of SIGBUS twice** on 2026-10-28, in the test process itself rather than the child it kills: SQLite reading its WAL index (`walIndexTryHdr`), which it maps from the LazyFS mount, while `verify` ran after a power cut. Once LazyFS aborted right after; once it stayed mounted. Both reruns passed all 60 rounds, and no data check failed. It came a third time on 2026-11-14, again in `verify` after a cut, 20 seconds into a run of five tests at once, and LazyFS aborted after it; the rerun passed. It came three times more: in the weekly runs of 2026-11-08 and 2026-11-22, in the child of `nibkeep-store`'s test in its first round, and on 2026-11-15 in the test process of `nibkeep-node`'s tests as they started; the reruns passed. Worth investigating: it now comes about once a week. *Mitigation:* keep the core dumps and read them; a guess to check first is the WAL index that SQLite maps from the FUSE mount, which a test process that lives through the cut keeps mapped, as no process on a real computer does.
- **Tests that failed once or twice and passed when run again,** each worth a look if it comes back:
  - `two_processes_write_an_encrypted_database_at_once_and_a_crash_harms_neither` (`crates/nibkeep-store/tests/crash.rs`) ran past its 60 s limit on CI's Linux runner ("the child wrote too little"), and failed the same way on CI's Windows runner on 2026-11-13; it takes 30 to 41 s on a desktop. *Mitigation:* a limit on how long the child goes without writing, rather than on the whole run.
  - `the_secrets_of_a_copy_are_taken_later_with_secret_from_copy` (`apps/nib/tests/init.rs`) failed on CI's Linux runner with `NoEntity("Bank")` after the device was made from the copy.
  - The tests of several programs on one replica (`several.rs`) on Windows, where a busy writer keeps another program's opening waiting past SQLite's 5 s. The burst that commits by itself, above, may end it.
  - `an_endpoint_takes_its_port_or_the_next_free_one` (`crates/nibkeep-net/tests/connections.rs`) on CI's Windows runner, which gave the endpoint none of the ten ports from 53391, likely as Windows had reserved them there. *Mitigation:* an endpoint that finds none of its ports takes one the system gives, which DNS-SD announces as any.
  - The end-to-end story of two devices listed the revoked desktop with its last sync instead of `(revoked)` alone, when every story ran at once: once as `(synced …; revoked)`, and on 2026-11-16 as `(last sync failed …: the other device is no replica of this library; revoked)`. Alone, it passed both times.

### What no test covers

- **The simulation writes no languages or forms** (`crates/nibkeep-node/tests/simulation.rs`).
- **The simulation's infected replica writes only part of what its key can sign** (`crates/nibkeep-node/tests/infected_simulation.rs`): changes of the personal space, by a device of the person's or an AI agent's replica, with any parents, times, kinds, keys, purges and statements, also on the replicas' records and in the field of passwords, sent whole or without their payload or key. It writes no secret statements or their keys, no blobs or their keys, no keys of its own making or changes signed with them, and no sync messages that misstate what it holds, and it is in no space with members. A hole there is found only by a review, or a test written by hand, such as `infected_in_groups.rs`. Its checks of what counts pass over a change sent without its payload or a key of the space, or whose payload does not fit its kind, which adds nothing, though it is not set aside either. *Mitigation:* each step that adds to what a device may write adds it to what this replica writes, and a family's spaces bring members, roles and groups to it before they go into daily use.
- **The end-to-end tests recover a library only from a backup, asking about each device kept:** not in place of a library that no longer unlocks, nor with `--keep`, which `nib`'s own tests cover in process. *Mitigation:* a story that forgets a library's passphrase and recovers in place, keeping a device with `--keep`.
- **On Windows, no end-to-end test types at a terminal,** so none sets up secrets, makes a recovery code or recovers a library there: the tests give `nib` a pseudo-terminal only on Unix ([technical design](Technical-design.md#18-end-to-end-tests)), and `nib` reads passphrases and codes on Windows from the console, through the same crate as on Unix. *Mitigation:* start such a command with `CreateProcessW` and a pseudo-console of its own, by hand, until std gives the attribute in stable Rust.
- **No test covers a code's secrets in a space reached through a group,** nor anyone's secrets there, nor recovering them: a code is given them by the same rule as where its member is in the space itself.
